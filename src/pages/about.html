About this prototype

This prototype was built for the 10 Downing Street data science hackathon, for the Ministry of Justice Domestic Abuse Gateway challenge. It is not a government service.

The problem

To get legal aid for a private family matter on the basis of domestic abuse, applicants must give evidence that meets Schedule 1 of the Civil Legal Aid (Procedure) Regulations 2012. Letters are often returned because of small wording problems, which are only found during caseworker review – weeks after the letter was written.

Our approach

Victims are usually the people who ask for evidence letters and pass them on. This service supports them at the 2 points where they can affect the result:

  1. When asking for a letter, by giving the professional a note with the exact wording needed.
  2. When a letter arrives, by checking it before it reaches the solicitor.

The same checks could run for providers and Legal Aid Agency caseworkers before an application is submitted.

How the checks work

The letter checker uses rules derived from the accepted and rejected example letters in the hackathon evidence pack, for paragraphs 11, 14, 17, 18 and 19. Every example is tested: accepted letters pass, and each rejected letter fails for the reason it was rejected. The tests also cover variations of the examples, such as "may be" or "could be" instead of "might be", and letters in capitals or with broken lines.

Letters from a MARAC, a refuge that gave someone a place, social services and banks or employers are also checked. There were no real examples of these, so their rules come from GOV.UK guidance and are tested against fictional letters only.

The demo page runs every test letter in the browser and shows the results.

Safety and privacy

  • Finding evidence, request notes, letter checks, the letter builder and the tracker run in the browser. There are no analytics or cookies. Nothing is stored on the device unless someone chooses to save their progress, and nothing is stored on the server.
  • Photos and PDFs of letters are read on the device. The text reader and its language data are served from this website.
  • The optional AI second opinion on a letter only starts after the person agrees. It downloads a model from Hugging Face and jsDelivr, and runs it on the device. The letter is not sent.
  • Exit this page is on every page, and pressing Shift 3 times leaves quickly.
  • The service never asks people to describe the abuse – only which services they have contacted.
  • The helper uses an OpenAI model for process questions. Emergency help, privacy questions and pasted letters are always answered by fixed rules on the device, and the scripted answers are used if the AI is unavailable.

What it can do

  • Check a letter covers all 31 types of evidence in Schedules 1 and 2, from health professional and support service letters to police, court and Home Office evidence.
  • It gets every letter in the hackathon evidence pack right, and 402 of 404 fictional letters it had not seen while the rules were tuned.
  • Health professional and support organisation letters, the most common types, are each tested against 29 example letters.
  • For each requirement it shows the sentence it relied on and what to ask the writer for, so a letter can be fixed before it reaches the Legal Aid Agency.
  • Where it matters, such as a finding of fact, it checks the evidence is dated before the legal aid application.

Next steps

  • Test against real, anonymised letters and Legal Aid Agency decisions, and add more of the ways professionals phrase the required wording.
  • Add example letters for the evidence types built from the guidance checklist.
  • Check letterheads, signatures and the author's professional registration.
  • Have the Welsh page checked by a translator, and translate the rest of the service.
  • It is a screening aid: people make the decisions.
